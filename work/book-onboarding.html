Onboarding advisor books at BMO Private Wealth

Incoming advisor books arrive against fixed transition dates. My job is to decide what clears, what goes back, and what the team works first, then to audit the finished files.

The problem

An incoming advisor’s book has to be onboarded by a fixed transition date. Each book is CAD $400M–$700M across 400+ client households, and one missing signature can stall it.

My contribution

I adjudicate new account applications against account-opening and KYC standards, track incoming asset transfers, build the daily case list for the team, and audit every completed file.

The result

My Associate Director’s written commendation for this work was forwarded to senior leadership. I have been in the role since September 2026, so I am not publishing performance figures yet.

Context

When an Investment Advisor joins, their clients’ accounts have to be opened and their assets transferred in by the transition date. Documentation moves between clients, Investment Advisors, Branch Operations and internal administration, and each hand-off is a place for a file to stall.

The figure of CAD $400M–$700M is the size of each advisor book, not a team total. Each book spans more than 400 client households.

My contribution

  • Adjudication. I test each new account application and its identity documentation against the account-opening and KYC standards, decide whether it clears, and return deficiencies to the advisor as a specific list.
  • Validation and transfers. I check client information across the internal banking systems, resolve discrepancies at source, and initiate and track incoming asset transfers, working rejections and exceptions through to resolution.
  • Sequencing. I build the prioritized case lists that set the team’s daily work, analyzing funds in transit at household level in Excel with pivot tables, lookups and Power Query.
  • Audit. I inspect every completed onboarding file against the standards and lead a group of colleagues through the audit cycle: assigning review work, consolidating findings and coordinating corrective action until each item is closed.

Where my decisions stop. I decide whether documentation meets the standard and in what order cases are worked. I do not advise clients or recommend products; the advisor owns the client relationship. I report to the Associate Director, Business Support and Onboarding.

A decision

How to order the queue. The simplest approach is first in, first out. I rank cases by asset value, case age and service-level risk instead, because a transition deadline is about assets landing and an old, large case that is close to its service level costs more to leave than a new, small one.

The cost of that choice is that small, recent cases wait longer. That is why the ranking has to be explainable case by case: I need to be able to say why one household is worked ahead of another.

Validation

Every completed file is audited against the account-opening, KYC and documentation standards before it is treated as finished, and each finding is tracked to rectification with the person responsible.

Limitations

  • I started in September 2026. This page describes responsibilities and method; it makes no claim about error rates, rankings or volumes beyond the size of a book.
  • It does not describe an individual case. Client matters are confidential, so I can talk through my reasoning in an interview but will not publish a specific file.
  • Nothing here reproduces BMO systems, data, policies or client information.
  • The linked models use invented data and simplified rules. They illustrate the reasoning, not BMO’s process.
