The Fifth Centre

A test-centre operator runs four sites and is considering a fifth. The build-out is expensive and the new site will open nearly empty. This is the investment case, built so the assumptions can be argued with.

The decision

Commit roughly $680K to a 24-seat centre that opens at 30% utilization and has to climb to pay for itself?

The method

Seven years of operating cash flows discounted at the hurdle rate give NPV and IRR. Solving for the utilization where NPV is zero gives the breakeven. Flexing each input shows which one matters.

The finding

On the base case the centre is worth building, but only just: breakeven utilization sits a few points under the plan. Utilization and price dominate everything else.

The recommendation

This is a utilization bet, not a construction bet. A 15% miss on build-out cost dents the return. A 15% miss on utilization destroys it. Diligence should go where the sensitivity is.

Secure the demand before the lease. Sponsor commitments covering the gap between breakeven and plan would turn a marginal case into a comfortable one. I would make approval conditional on them.

The ramp is the hidden risk. Each extra year spent reaching steady utilization costs more than a 15% overrun on the build-out. Opening with trained staff and a booked first quarter is worth paying for.

I have seen utilization move from 30% to 80% at existing centres, which is why I trust the upside here and also why I would not assume it.
