Why build it

Most valuation projects are a DCF with a growth rate typed in. I wanted one where changing a single operating driver moves all three statements, the valuation and the buyout returns together.

How it is being built

Every figure will be entered from the filings and every formula will be mine, so I can explain any cell without notes. The empty workbook structure and its check cells were generated with Claude; from here AI is used only to review finished tabs for broken links and hard-codes.

Why Air Canada

An airline is a deliberately hard case: heavy leases, fuel exposure, tax losses from the pandemic and customers who pay before they fly. Each one needs its own schedule.

Four things an airline forces you to get right

Leases. Aircraft leases sit on the balance sheet under IFRS but inside operating costs for US peers. The treatment has to be chosen once and applied the same way to EBITDA, to free cash flow and to the bridge from enterprise value to equity.

Tax losses. Losses carried forward mean cash taxes differ from the tax expense for several years, so the model tracks the loss pool.

Advance ticket sales. Passengers pay before they fly, so working capital is negative and growth generates cash. A percentage-of-revenue shortcut would miss it.

Buyout realism. Debt and leases are already several times EBITDA and Canadian law limits foreign ownership of an airline. The LBO is a modelling exercise, and the write-up will say so.

The workbook and write-up will be posted here when the model balances and the valuation is complete. Until then there is nothing to download, because there is nothing finished to check.
