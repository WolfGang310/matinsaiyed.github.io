Month-end close as a critical path

A close is a set of tasks with dependencies, so it has a critical path. This project models a six-day close for an invented services firm, then builds the deferred-revenue schedule that feeds it. The six days belong to the model, not to an employer.

Four contracts with different terms and billing patterns. Pick a month to see what was billed, what was earned, the balance roll-forward and the entries to book.

Synthetic data An independent project built with AI assistance. No client or employer information is used, and no figure describes a real firm.

Try it

Twelve close tasks, each with a duration and the tasks it waits on. Press + on any task to delay it by half a day.

What to notice

Delaying a dark task pushes the close past day six. Delaying a light one is absorbed until its hatched slack runs out.

The control

In the revenue engine, the roll-forward always ties: opening balance plus billings minus revenue recognized equals closing balance.

Why build it this way

A close calendar is only useful if it shows what cannot slip. Seven of the twelve tasks sit on the critical path here. Knowing which five have slack tells you where a late bank feed or a slow approval can be tolerated.

Schedules should produce their own entries. The deferred-revenue engine derives each month’s journal entries from the contract terms, so the entry, the schedule and the balance cannot disagree.

Every balance gets a roll-forward. It is the fastest way to prove a number and the first thing a reviewer asks for.
